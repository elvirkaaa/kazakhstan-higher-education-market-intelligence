## Purpose

This brief distils Kazakhstan's higher education market into the essentials an international reader needs in two pages: what the market looks like, what the data shows, and where the clearest opportunities and risks sit. Full evidence, methodology, and citations are in the companion full report.

## Dataset

A nationally sourced registry of academic programmes (OPVO/EPVO classification), covering **46 universities** and **8,530 programme-level records**, resolving to **≈2,417 distinct programmes** after deduplication, across **4 degree levels** and **11 subject areas**, priced in KZT for domestic and international students.

## Key Findings

1. Almaty holds **≈72%** of all distinct programmes and 20 of 46 universities — the market is effectively city-concentrated, not nationally distributed.
2. English-medium instruction covers **35%** of programmes nationally, but ranges from **78%** (ICT) to **10%** (Agriculture) by field.
3. Tuition rises consistently with degree level: Bachelor's (median ≈1.0M KZT) → Master's (≈1.28M) → Doctoral (≈1.9M).
4. The English-medium price premium is real on average but **reverses in ~1 of every 3** comparable subject/level pairs — no universal pricing rule applies.
5. Business, Economics & Law and Engineering & Technology are the most contested fields (27 and 26 providers); Architecture & Urban Planning has only **5** providers nationally.
6. Master's/Doctoral provision outside Almaty is very thin — the clearest quantified regional gap in the dataset.
7. Institutions split into two structural models — large generalists or narrow single-subject specialists — with little stable middle ground.
8. 13 of 43 priced institutions show **zero price variation** across their entire catalogue, a data-quality flag for competitor benchmarking.
9. Regional price differences are modest (≈3x across regions); regional *availability* differences are far more extreme — the market's inequality is one of catalogue breadth, not price.
10. Classification codes (OPVO) are **not unique** across institutions — the same code has been found attached to unrelated programmes at different universities.
11. ~45% of all region × subject-area combinations have zero recorded programmes — extensive, if unverified, supply-side white space.
12. All findings are supply-side only: this registry contains no enrolment or demand data, so every "gap" is an absence of current provision, not confirmed demand.

## Strategic Insights (summary)

- **Almaty is the market**, not one region among several — entry strategy should be scoped city-first.
- **English-medium readiness is field-specific** — partnership friction will differ sharply by discipline.
- **Pricing premiums are not universal** — benchmark by subject/level, not by a national rule.
- **The market has no stable "mid-sized generalist" model** — entrants should choose specialist or broad, deliberately.
- **Graduate education outside Almaty is the clearest white space** identified in this analysis.
- **The most obvious fields (Business, Engineering) are also the most saturated** — thin-provider fields deserve equal consideration.
- **A meaningful share of listed pricing may be flat, not differentiated** — treat competitor prices with care.
