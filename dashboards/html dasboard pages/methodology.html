Key finding

The two largest data-integrity risks in this project — programme duplication in the OPVO/EPVO registry and regional double-counting across Kazakhstan's 2022 administrative boundary reform — were both identified and corrected before use, and are documented here rather than hidden.

Analytical TaxonomyWhat kind of claim is this?

Every statement on this site falls into one of six categories. Confusing them — presenting an interpretation as a fact, or a hypothesis as a recommendation — is the most common way analysis overstates itself. One worked example per category, using real numbers from this project:

CategoryDefinitionExample from this project
Observed factDirectly measured, in the source data, no calculation required"Births fell from 445,875 (2021) to 335,005 (2025)" — read directly from BNS tables
Derived metricCalculated from observed facts using a defined, checkable formula"Regional HHI = 5,176" — sum of squared regional programme shares
InterpretationWhat a derived metric plausibly means, stated with its own caveat"The market is more geographically concentrated than institutionally concentrated" — a reading of the HHI comparison, not a new measurement
HypothesisAn untested explanation the data cannot itself confirm or rule out"Almaty may serve demand drawn from other regions" — plausible, but would require student origin data this project does not have
RecommendationA suggested next action, explicitly downstream of the evidence, never stated as certain"Monitor the six above-median-youth, below-median-supply regions for further investigation" — action framed as a next step, not a conclusion
LimitationA stated boundary on what the data or method can support"This dataset cannot show that migrants are students, or that they migrated for education-related reasons at all"

This taxonomy is applied throughout the site via consistent phrasing — "this may indicate," "this does not show," "a plausible hypothesis" — rather than a label on every sentence. The Supply × Demand and Strategic Implications pages make the heaviest use of the distinction.

Case Study 01Regional boundary versioning in Power BI

Kazakhstan's regional migration data spans two administrative structures: pre-2022 (14-17 units) and post-2022 (20 regions). Early in Power BI model-building, before a region crosswalk was applied, migration values from both boundary versions were loaded side by side — meaning several regions were counted twice under two different names.

This is a real intermediate state from this project's own Power BI work, reproduced here with its actual values (not illustrative placeholder data) — including the exact figures for Abay (-9,856), Akmola (+292), and Aktobe (-4,216) that first revealed the problem.

Known LimitationsAcross this project

  • ~15 of 2,417 programmes (0.6%) are not attributed to a specific city
  • Reference year for interregional migration is inferred, not stated in source
  • No source tracks where in Kazakhstan foreign students study
  • Official sources disagree on CIS-vs-Asia origin totals for 2024: one infographic states CIS 17,816 / Asia 17,788; the National Report's chart states CIS 15,816 / Asia 17,456; its narrative text states CIS 12,816 — three figures across publications by the same ministry, all preserved rather than reconciled
  • Bolashak level-breakdown sums to 143 fewer than the cumulative total
  • 13 institutions show zero tuition variance — flat fee vs. unpopulated field is unresolvable
