Supply × Demand

Where do demand and supply diverge?

Youth population and higher-education programme supply, region by region — combining two independent datasets to see where they visibly do not line up.

Data period: WP1 2026 × WP3 2024/25 · Regions: post-2022 (20)
Central finding

Higher youth-population concentration does not correspond to higher local higher-education provision. The regions with the largest young populations — Turkistan, Mangystau, Shymkent city — hold almost none of the country's programme supply, whereas Almaty and East Kazakhstan, with below-average youth shares, hold the most. This is a spatial relationship; it does not, on its own, demonstrate causation.

02 · Testing the RelationshipHow strong is the pattern, actually?

A visual pattern is not the same as a tested relationship. Pearson's r (sensitive to the Almaty outlier) and Spearman's rho (rank-based, more robust to outliers) are reported together — where they disagree sharply, that disagreement is itself informative.

RelationshipPearson rSpearman ρReading
Youth share ↔ HE supply share-0.19-0.36Weak, slightly negative — no support for "more youth → more supply"
Natural growth ↔ HE supply share-0.05-0.21Essentially no relationship
Net migration ↔ HE supply share+0.36+0.01Pearson suggests a weak positive link; Spearman shows almost none — the correlation is driven by one or two extreme regions (Astana, Almaty), not a general pattern

Correlation coefficients do not establish causation, and with only 20 regional observations, these results are indicative rather than statistically decisive. The principal finding here is a negative one: none of the three demographic indicators tested shows a relationship with programme supply that is both meaningful in magnitude and robust to the removal of outlying regions.

Regional Alignment Index

A screening indicator, not a market-opportunity score: for each region, the average z-score of demand-side measures (youth share, natural growth) minus the average z-score of supply-side measures (programme share, programme count). Positive = demand pressure relatively high, supply relatively low. Computed directly from the values already shown above — no new data introduced.

This index identifies where demographic pressure and recorded supply diverge most strongly — it does not measure unmet demand, market opportunity, or capacity shortfall, none of which this project's data can establish. Turkistan (+1.82) and Almaty (-4.57) are the two clearest exceptions to a simple "more youth → more supply" expectation — see their profiles below.

05 · ScopeWhat this analysis does and does not show

This analysis shows

  • Where youth population and HE programme supply are co-located, and where they are not
  • Which regions combine high youth share with low supply
  • Which regions combine high net in-migration with either high or low supply

This analysis does not show

  • Why supply is concentrated where it is (causality)
  • Whether migrants are students, or migrate for education-related reasons at all
  • Unmet demand in any quantified sense — programme registrations are not student enrolments
  • Physical or financial accessibility of existing supply
  • Household income, tuition affordability, transport, or student preference — none are captured in this dataset
  • A single consistent year: supply data is 2026, demographic data is 2024-2025 — treated as roughly concurrent, not identical
Why this matters for AZM

Regions combining a young, growing population with limited local supply are plausible candidates for further market investigation — expansion or capacity development among them. Confirming that requires demand-side research (applications, enrolment intentions, affordability) that this dataset does not contain.
