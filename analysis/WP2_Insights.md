# WP2 Insights

Insight-mapping stage: each row is a candidate finding for the eventual WP2 report, traced back to the evidence and source(s) that support it, with a confidence rating. Confidence reflects how many independent sources agree, whether the underlying numbers arithmetically reconcile, and how many years of data back the claim — not how interesting the finding is.

**Confidence key:** *High* = confirmed by 2+ independent sources and/or internally reconciling multi-year data. *Medium* = a single well-evidenced source, or a pattern visible in only one year. *Low* = suggestive but thin, contradictory, or unresolved evidence.

| # | Insight | Evidence | Source(s) | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Most international students in Kazakhstan come from CIS/neighbouring countries, with Uzbekistan and Russia consistently the top two | CIS report (Uzbekistan 9,571 enrolled students, 2022), National Report (CIS dominates inbound mobility every year 2021-2024), UNDESA (Uzbekistan and Russia are Kazakhstan's top two migrant-stock origin countries) | CIS + National Report + UNDESA | High |
| 2 | India is the dominant non-CIS source country, and is almost entirely concentrated in medical fields | National Report: India leads all countries in 2024 (12,020 students), of which 11,997 (99.8%) are at medical-focused universities; Bologna Report 2023 independently notes English-medium foreign students are concentrated in Health care, citing India specifically | National Report + Bologna Report 2023 | High |
| 3 | Kazakhstan's elite state-funded outbound scholars (Bolashak) and its general inbound foreign student population go to/come from almost opposite parts of the world | Bolashak cumulative destinations: UK/Ireland 45.5%, USA/Canada 28% (73.5% combined, Anglophone West); inbound foreign students: overwhelmingly CIS and Asia (India, Uzbekistan, Russia) | National Report (Bolashak + inbound mobility sections, same document) | High |

## Notes on using this table

- Insights rated *Medium* or below should not be presented as settled findings in the final WP2 report without either (a) an additional confirming source, or (b) an additional year of data once available.
- Insight #11 is a meta-finding about the evidence base itself, not a substantive claim about Kazakhstan's higher education system — it belongs in the report's Limitations section, not the Findings section, but is included here because it is itself a well-evidenced, mappable insight from this stage of work.
- Several insights (#3, #6) point toward the same underlying pattern — a bifurcation between elite/state-funded outbound internationalisation (Western-oriented) and organic inbound internationalisation (CIS/Asia-oriented) — and could be merged into a single, stronger insight in the final report rather than presented as two separate points.
- No insight in this table currently draws on UNESCO UIS data, since that source has not yet been independently verified (see `docs/wp2/Data_Sources.md`) — once verified, it may either reinforce or complicate insights #1, #5, and #7 above, particularly for cross-country comparison.
- A third source (MSHE Foreign University Partnerships, 2025) has since been added, reinforcing Insight #11's data-reliability concern rather than resolving it - see Insight #15.
